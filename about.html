About this site

Worthing Brighton Bus shows live buses and trains along the Adur, Worthing and Brighton coast, works out what a journey actually costs in tickets, and sets out a case for better service across the council boundary at Shoreham.

It is independent. It is not run by, funded by, or endorsed by any bus operator, train operator, or council. Operator names, route numbers and liveries appear here to identify services — that is all they mean. Where the site quotes a fare or a departure count, it says where the figure came from and when it was checked.

Correcting something

If something here is wrong, please say so — a campaign site that gets its facts wrong is worse than no campaign site. The quickest route is to open an issue on the public issue tracker, which is where the project's work happens in the open.

That includes asking for something to be taken down. If you submitted an idea, a route or a stop report and want it removed, say so on the same tracker and it will be removed from the site. Note that a submission also creates a public issue, so ask for that to be deleted too and it will be.

What this site does with your information

Submissions
Ideas, route proposals and stop reports are sent to a Cloudflare Worker, which files them as public issues on GitHub straight away — before anyone has reviewed them. A person then decides whether they appear on the site. "Moderated before appearing here" is not the same as "private until approved", so please don't put anything in a submission you would not want published, including your email address. The forms don't ask for one.
The name you put on a submission
Optional, and published with it if you give one.
Rate limiting
To stop the submission form being abused, your IP address is hashed with a secret and a counter is kept against that hash for up to two days. The address itself is never stored.
Postcode lookup
"Email your councillor" sends the postcode you type to postcodes.io to find which ward and electoral division you are in. Nothing is stored here and nothing is sent anywhere else. The draft letter is composed in your browser and handed to your own email app — this site never sees it and never sends it.
Things kept in your browser
Your theme choice, your filter settings and any unsent route draft are stored locally on your device. They stay there and are not transmitted.
Other services your browser contacts
Map tiles from OpenStreetMap, fonts from Google Fonts, the map library from unpkg, an anti-spam challenge from Cloudflare Turnstile on the forms, and this project's own API on Render for live departures. Each of those sees your IP address, as any site you visit does. There is no analytics and there are no advertising trackers.

Accessibility

The site aims at WCAG 2.2 AA, with 44 px targets and AAA text contrast where it can manage them. Known gaps, stated rather than hidden: the map itself is difficult to operate without a pointer, which is why the live view carries a stop and station search that does the same job from the keyboard. If something is unusable for you, that is a bug — please report it on the tracker and say what you were trying to do.

Where the data comes from

Timetables from the Department for Transport's Bus Open Data Service, rebuilt weekly. Live vehicle positions from the same service. Train times from Realtime Trains. Maps © OpenStreetMap contributors. Boundary geography from the ONS Open Geography Portal under the Open Government Licence. Councillor names and addresses come from each council's own published member directory and are never derived from a name pattern.

Service comparisons on this site count scheduled departures from the published timetable. That is not the same as buses that actually ran, and a difference in service either side of a boundary is not on its own proof that the boundary caused it. The full method, denominators and caveats are published with the figures.
